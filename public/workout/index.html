This week’s split

Why it rotates

Each weekday keeps a fixed job — Monday is always back, Wednesday is always chest, Thursday is always shoulders and arms. What changes is the selection: exercises are drawn from a pool using the ISO week number, so the stimulus moves every Monday without you replanning anything.
Because the draw is seeded by the week, it’s stable within the week — reload mid-session and you get the same workout, not a new one. Shuffle rerolls a single day when you want variety sooner.
Every 4th week is a deload: same movements, roughly 20% less load, slower tempo, rep ranges treated as a ceiling. It’s what keeps the knee work and the endurance volume sustainable across months.

The four constants

Posture — scapular retraction, thoracic extension and neck positioning appear every single week, because posture responds to frequency far more than to intensity.
Core — weighted toward anti-rotation and anti-extension (Pallof, dead bug, bird dog) over flexion volume. That’s what actually transfers to standing posture and to knee control.
ACL-safe knee — split across two days: one strength day (bridges, TKEs, controlled step-ups) and one balance day (single-leg stance, banded lateral work, slow step-downs), since post-ACL knees lose proprioception as much as strength.
Muscle without bulk — four lifting days a week (back, chest, shoulders and arms, plus posterior chain) in the 8–15 rep hypertrophy range rather than 1–5 rep max work. Enough volume to visibly add muscle, not enough to carry you toward a heavyweight build.
Endurance — steady-state and intervals stay in every week, both because you asked for the engine and because the conditioning is what keeps body fat low enough for the muscle to actually show.

Track the waist, not just the scale. Measure at the navel once a week, same time of day, first thing. Love handles are a circumference problem, and waist centimetres will move while the scale sits still for days at a time. If weight drops but the waist doesn’t, the deficit is too aggressive and you’re losing muscle rather than fat.

Your current priority

You cannot spot-reduce the flanks. No amount of oblique work removes fat from the area it trains — and loaded side bends actively thicken the waist, which is the opposite of what you want. The core work here stays anti-rotation and anti-extension for exactly that reason. Love handles are the last place most men lose fat, so they go late in a cut, not early.
Lift heavy while you cut. The instinct in a deficit is to chase lighter weights and higher reps to “tone”. That is precisely how you lose muscle. Keep the loads where they are and let the kitchen create the deficit — the training’s job for the next ten weeks is to give your body a reason to keep the muscle it has.
Play to the wide back. If the back is your strongest feature, it needs maintaining, not more volume — Monday already does that. The taper is a ratio, so the leverage now sits at the other two ends: side delts, which get four slots a week, and the waist, which the cut handles. Wide back plus capped delts plus a tight waist is the entire silhouette.
The training side that matters most for that silhouette is shoulders and back width against a tight waist. That’s why lateral raises and pull-ups get the highest rep volume in the whole program, and why core work leans anti-rotation rather than heavy weighted side bends that thicken the waist.
